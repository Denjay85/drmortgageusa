Mortgage Servicing Transfers in Florida

By Dennis Ross, NMLS #2018381|November 17, 2026

A letter says your mortgage payment now goes to a different company. Before you change a bank instruction or click a payment link, verify the transfer. For Orlando homeowners, my recommendation is simple: confirm who should receive the next payment, when that changes, and whether your payment setup needs attention.

First, Confirm Who Is Collecting the Payment

Your mortgage servicer is the company handling your monthly payments, and that company can change. The CFPB explains that a servicing transfer means payments and loan questions move to the new servicer after the applicable date.[1] Do not treat the arrival of a new company name as a reason to refinance.

My recommendation is to call your current servicer to confirm the new company before changing the payment destination. I would start with the contact information on a statement you already trust, rather than a phone number in an unexpected text. Ask the current servicer to verify the transfer and the destination for your next payment.

Read the Dates, Not Just the New Logo

The CFPB says transfer notices should identify the effective transfer date, when the old servicer stops accepting payments, when the new one starts, and the new company's contact information.[1] My suggestion is to put those dates beside your next payment due date and check the actual instructions before changing anything.

The current federal regulation generally calls for the old servicer's notice at least 15 days before transfer and the new servicer's notice no more than 15 days afterward. A combined notice generally goes out at least 15 days before transfer. Exceptions and settlement-delivered notices exist.[2] These are general federal rules, not a separate Florida deadline or a determination about your particular notice.

Do Not Assume Autopay Is Ready

The CFPB specifically tells borrowers using their bank or credit union's online bill-pay system to redirect those payments to the new servicer.[1] Bank bill pay and a debit arranged directly with a mortgage servicer are different setups. My recommendation is to ask both companies what happens to your existing authorization, rather than assume either one transfers automatically.

  • Confirm the correct account number and payment address through a verified channel.
  • Ask whether a scheduled payment is still pending before arranging another one.
  • Confirm when any new automatic payment starts and how to handle the next due date.
  • Save the instructions and the payment confirmation in your own records.

Do not cancel a pending payment or send a duplicate just to make an unfamiliar portal look current. Ask the servicer to explain what is scheduled and what it has received. If you are still organizing payments after a recent closing, see first mortgage payment timing in Florida.

The 60-Day Protection Is Not a Payment Holiday

Under the current regulation, during the 60-day period beginning on the transfer's effective date, a payment received by the old servicer on or before the applicable due date, including any contractual grace period, may not be treated as late. The rule also prohibits a late fee in that situation.[2] The protection is tied to an on-time payment received by the old servicer. It is not permission to skip payments for two months.

If you already paid the old company, I would gather the confirmation and ask both servicers to trace the payment. Keep your account details private. Do not post an unredacted statement or bank screenshot in a public comment while trying to get help.

Check the First Statement and Keep the Records

I would keep statements, payment records, and communications together. My suggested check is to compare the new statement with your last one: payment credited, outstanding balance, escrow information, and any unfamiliar charge. Ask for an explanation of a mismatch instead of guessing why it appeared.

The transfer notice must state that servicing transfer does not change loan terms other than terms directly related to servicing.[2] That does not mean you should ignore a different total payment. Ask the servicer to identify the reason and provide the relevant statement or notice. For the separate escrow conversation, read home insurance renewals and mortgage escrow in Florida.

If the Transfer Does Not Add Up, Ask for Help

The CFPB points borrowers with missing notices, incorrectly applied payments, or transfer-related assistance problems toward its information-request and notice-of-error resources.[1] Use the official resource and confirm the servicer's designated address and process. For a disputed legal right, deadline, or threatened foreclosure, get qualified legal help rather than relying on a blog checklist.

My bottom line: verify the company, confirm the date, check the payment setup, and keep proof. A new payment destination deserves a careful review, not a rushed click.
