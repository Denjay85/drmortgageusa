Florida Home Insurance Renewal and Escrow

By Dennis Ross, NMLS #2018381|November 5, 2026

Your renewal arrives, and another insurer offers a lower premium. Before you switch, I would separate two questions: is the coverage appropriate, and who is handling the insurance bill? For an Orlando homeowner with mortgage escrow, I would review both before authorizing a change.

The CFPB explains that mortgage escrow collects part of your monthly payment for expenses such as property taxes and insurance. The servicer pays those bills, and changes in those costs can change your total monthly payment.[1] That makes a renewal more than a quote comparison.

Compare Coverage Before Comparing the Price

Florida's Department of Financial Services recommends reviewing deductibles and asking whether coverage uses replacement cost or actual cash value. A higher deductible can lower the premium while leaving you responsible for more when you file a claim.[2]

My recommendation is to put the renewal and proposed policy side by side with a licensed insurance agent. Ask the agent to explain differences in dwelling coverage, roof loss settlement, hurricane deductibles, exclusions, and additional living expenses. Ask what you would have to pay yourself after a covered loss. Do not assume a cheaper quote buys the same protection.

I handle the mortgage questions, not insurance placement. Have your insurance professional explain the actual policy terms and have your servicer confirm its insurance requirements for your loan.

Ask the Servicer What It Needs Before Switching

I would contact the servicer through the number on your statement or its authenticated portal. My checklist would be:

  • Has the current renewal already been paid from escrow, or is payment scheduled?
  • What policy documents and mortgagee information should the insurance agent provide?
  • Where should the replacement policy and invoice be sent, and how will receipt be confirmed?
  • Who needs to confirm the new premium has been paid?

These are process questions, not a promise that every servicer uses the same workflow. Save the answers and confirmations. I would coordinate the effective dates with the agent and servicer before requesting cancellation, rather than assume everyone received the same information.

Keep Proof of Coverage in the Process

The CFPB says a servicer may require force-placed insurance if your own policy is missing, lapses, or does not meet your mortgage contract's requirements. It is usually more expensive and often protects only the lender. The agency recommends sending proof of your own coverage to the servicer.[3]

My recommendation: get confirmation that the replacement policy meets the servicer's requirements, confirm its effective date with the insurer, and keep evidence of delivery. If you receive an insurance warning while making the switch, contact the servicer and agent promptly. Do not ignore it because you already obtained a quote.

Do Not Budget a Lower Mortgage Payment Yet

I would not reduce a scheduled mortgage payment based on a cheaper insurance proposal. Ask the servicer whether an updated escrow analysis is needed, when any revised payment would take effect, and whether an existing shortage remains. Use the required amount on the servicer's current instructions until it confirms a change.

For a separate look at payment increases, read Florida escrow shortages and payment changes. If you are still buying, see homeowners insurance and mortgage approval in Florida.

Ask About Any Refund Instead of Assuming It Is Spending Money

If the old insurer issues a refund, ask it to explain the amount and ask the servicer how the policy change affects your escrow account. My recommendation is to keep that money available until you understand whether any escrow deposit or other payment is needed. This article does not establish a refund amount, cancellation fee, or universal refund-handling rule.

Check the Follow-Through

The CFPB recommends monitoring mortgage statements and insurance bills and contacting the servicer promptly about escrow problems.[4] I would check the next statement against the written confirmations, not just the quoted premium. Did the intended insurer get paid? Does the servicer show the right policy? Is the amount due what the servicer told you to expect?

My bottom line: compare the coverage with your insurance agent, confirm the payment process with your servicer, and keep the paperwork until the change is reflected correctly. A lower premium is worth reviewing. It is not permission to guess about your next mortgage payment.
