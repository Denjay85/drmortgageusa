Pay Off Your Car Before Buying a Florida Home?

By Dennis Ross, NMLS #2018381|October 12, 2026

You have enough savings to pay off the car, and you want to buy a home in Orlando. My recommendation: compare the home purchase with the auto loan and without it before you move the money.

Do not make this a simple "debt is bad" decision. Ask which problem the purchase plan actually needs to solve: the monthly obligation, the money needed to close, or the cash you want left afterward. Start with a lender review, not a transfer.

Why Your Car Loan Matters to Mortgage Qualification

For mortgages subject to Fannie Mae's rules, auto loans are installment debt. The guide requires lenders to count these obligations when more than ten monthly payments remain. Shorter remaining terms can still count if the payment significantly affects the borrower's ability to meet credit obligations.[1]

Do not assume being near the end of the loan makes the payment irrelevant. Ask your loan officer to confirm how your actual obligation is being treated. For background, read debt-to-income ratio for Florida homebuyers.

These are Fannie Mae requirements, not a universal rule for every conventional, FHA, VA, or other mortgage. Have the lender verify your program and any additional lender requirements before choosing a payoff strategy.

Paying Off Debt Is Part of the Loan Review, Not an Approval Shortcut

Fannie Mae says a payoff or paydown done solely to qualify must be evaluated in the overall loan analysis, including the borrower's credit-use history. Its guide generally allows installment loans being paid off, or paid down to ten or fewer remaining payments, to be excluded from long-term debt.[2]

Read that alongside the shorter-term-debt caution above. It is not permission to exclude a payment yourself. My recommendation is to ask the lender to review the proposed payoff and explain the resulting qualification before you commit the cash.

Also identify what you are paying: Fannie Mae treats automobile lease payments as recurring obligations regardless of the remaining lease term. A lease is not interchangeable with an auto loan for this analysis.[1]

Keep the Cash Decision Separate From the Debt Decision

The CFPB recommends setting aside money for moving, other savings goals, and an emergency cushion before deciding how much cash is available for closing. It also separates closing costs from the down payment.[3]

Use that distinction when asking for a comparison. I would want both versions on the same page: keep the auto loan, and pay it off. Use the same target home and the same verified loan assumptions so the comparison answers the actual question.

  • Qualification: Ask what monthly obligations the lender counts in each version and whether the proposed financing remains workable.
  • Purchase cash: Ask for the down payment, closing costs, and any lender-required reserves in each version.
  • Cash afterward: Separately identify the money you choose to keep for emergencies, moving, and initial home expenses.
  • Personal comfort: Decide whether the remaining monthly obligations and remaining savings fit your life, not just a loan calculation.

My recommendation is to avoid treating every dollar in your bank account as available for the car payoff. First decide what needs to stay available for the purchase and what you want untouched afterward. For related planning, read Florida mortgage reserves and why preapproval is not your real budget.

Compare the Full Ownership Budget

The CFPB's affordability guidance includes other monthly debts and property costs, not just mortgage principal and interest. It calls out taxes, insurance, HOA fees, utilities, maintenance, and repairs as costs buyers need to consider.[3]

For your Orlando purchase, I recommend gathering property-specific estimates instead of deciding from a generic mortgage calculator. Put those estimates beside the auto-loan comparison. Ask yourself which version gives you a budget you can maintain and savings you are comfortable keeping.

What to Ask Before Sending the Payoff

Here is the short conversation I recommend having with your loan officer:

  1. Which loan program are we reviewing, and how does it treat this auto obligation?
  2. Can you compare keeping the loan with the proposed payoff using the same homebuying assumptions?
  3. What payoff amount and account documents should I obtain, and what proof will you need?
  4. Should the payoff happen before closing or be coordinated with closing, if the lender accepts that approach?
  5. What cash needs to remain available, and what still needs underwriting review?

Ask about documentation and timing before transferring funds. This article does not promise a credit-score increase, an immediate credit-report update, a larger approval, or a particular closing date. Those are not assumptions to build your purchase around.

The Bottom Line for Florida Buyers

My recommendation is clear: do the comparison first. If the lender's review supports a payoff and you are comfortable with the cash remaining, consider that option. If the review shows you can keep the loan and you prefer to preserve savings, discuss that option instead. Do not pay off the car just because someone online said it is required.
