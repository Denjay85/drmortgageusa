How to Remove PMI in Florida

By Dennis Ross, NMLS #2018381|November 20, 2026

Your mortgage statement still shows private mortgage insurance, but your Orlando home might be worth more than when you bought it. Should you pay for an appraisal? Not yet. First find out which value your servicer will use and whether it already has a date for PMI to end.

Make Sure the Charge Is Actually PMI

Private mortgage insurance, or PMI, protects the lender. It is not the homeowners insurance that covers your property. On a conventional mortgage, a monthly PMI premium can appear under Projected Payments on page 1 of your Closing Disclosure. Compare that with your latest statement and ask your servicer to confirm what you are paying now.[1]

This checklist covers borrower-paid PMI on covered conventional loans for single-family primary residences. The same rules do not apply to FHA mortgage insurance, VA loans, or lender-paid mortgage insurance. Some high-risk loans also have exceptions. If you are not sure what you have, start by asking your servicer to name the loan and the coverage in writing.[2][3]

Asking at 80 Percent Is Not the Same as Waiting for 78 Percent

Borrower request: For covered loans, you can generally request cancellation when your principal balance is scheduled to reach 80 percent of the home's original value. Extra principal payments can get your actual balance there early and let you request cancellation sooner. The request is in writing. You generally need to be current and have a good payment history. The servicer can require certification that there is no junior lien and evidence that the property value has not fallen below its original value.[2][3]

Automatic termination: Generally, this uses the date your balance is scheduled to reach 78 percent of the original value, provided you are current. Paying extra does not move that scheduled date forward. There is also a separate midpoint rule for covered loans if PMI has not already ended and you are current. Ask your servicer for the dates it has on file rather than trying to reverse-engineer them from your last statement.[2][3]

For a purchase, original value generally means the lower of the contract price and the appraisal at purchase. If you refinanced, it generally means the appraisal at the time of that refinance. A current online estimate is not either of those numbers.[2] If you have been paying ahead, check that the money actually went to extra mortgage principal before assuming you are at the request threshold.

If You Want to Use Today's Orlando Home Value

Ask about that separately. Some loan investors have their own cancellation standards, so there may be another route even when you have not met the original-value threshold. A neighbor's sale is a reason to ask, not a reason to order your own appraisal. Get the servicer's requirements first, including what kind of valuation it accepts, who orders it, and who pays for it.[2]

What I Would Ask the Servicer

  1. What original value, current principal balance, and scheduled termination date are you using?
  2. Can I request cancellation now using original value? If not, is there a separate review based on current value?
  3. What paperwork and valuation do you require, and is there a fee before I know the result?
  4. If PMI ends, when will that show on my statement, and when should I update any payment I schedule through my bank?

Keep paying what your statement shows until the servicer confirms a change. If a refinance is on the table, compare keeping your current loan against replacing it, including any new closing costs and how long you expect to keep the loan. For FHA mortgage insurance, this is a different discussion; see refinancing from FHA to conventional in Florida.
