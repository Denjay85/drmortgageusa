VA Seller Credits in Florida: Beyond 4%

By Dennis Ross • Published May 24, 2026 Updated October 20, 2026

If you are buying a home in Florida with a VA loan, the seller may be able to help with more than you have been told. VA's 4% seller-concession limit is not a blanket ceiling on all seller-paid help. Understanding that distinction can open up a more useful conversation about your offer, your closing costs, and whether a buydown belongs in the plan. [1]

In my work with veterans, I have structured total seller-paid help of 6% to almost 7%, including buydowns. That is my professional experience, not a promise that your transaction can reach those numbers. The opportunity comes from matching eligible costs to the right categories, not getting VA to waive its actual concession cap.

Before you limit your request to 4%, let's look at what you actually need the seller's help to accomplish.

Two Categories, Not One Total-Help Ceiling

In a contract conversation, “seller credit” usually means the amount you are asking the seller to contribute. For VA's concession test, the lender must look at what that money pays for. A single negotiated credit can cover items in two different categories. [1]

  • Outside the 4% concession calculation: the seller's payment of normal buyer closing costs and discount points appropriate to the market.
  • Inside the 4% calculation: benefits VA classifies as seller concessions, including seller-funded temporary buydown escrow, extra points for a permanent buydown, the buyer's VA funding fee, prepaid buyer property taxes and insurance, and payoff of the buyer's credit balances or judgments.

The combined concessions in that second category cannot exceed 4% of the property's VA-established reasonable value, reflected in the Notice of Value, or NOV. It is not a choice between the contract price and the appraised value. If the purchase price and NOV differ, the lender must use the VA-established reasonable value for this cap. [1]

That leaves room for total seller help above 4% when ordinary allowable closing costs and market-appropriate points sit outside the capped category. It does not create a separate VA entitlement to 6%, 7%, or any other total contribution. [2]

What That Could Look Like in Dollars

Hypothetical allocation only: assume both the purchase price and the VA-established reasonable value are $400,000. Assume the lender has verified the following eligible costs and classifications. These are assumed amounts, not a fee estimate, pricing quote, or actual veteran's transaction.

  • $12,000 outside the cap: normal allowable buyer closing costs plus market-appropriate discount points.
  • $14,000 inside the cap: the combined eligible concessions, which could include seller-funded temporary buydown escrow and prepaid buyer property taxes and insurance.
  • $16,000 concession ceiling: 4% of the $400,000 VA-established reasonable value.
  • $26,000 total seller help: $12,000 plus $14,000, equal to 6.5% of the assumed purchase price.

The total is above 4%, but the concessions subject to the cap remain below $16,000. That is the distinction that matters. This example does not establish that your costs will support the same allocation, that a particular buydown is available, or that a seller will agree to it.

Buydowns Can Be Part of the Strategy

A buydown deserves its own review because temporary and permanent options do not work the same way. The word “buydown” alone does not tell us how VA will classify the seller's contribution.

A temporary buydown uses money set aside in escrow to subsidize payments for a limited period. When the seller funds that escrow, it counts toward the 4% concession cap. You must qualify using the full payment due after the subsidy ends, not just the reduced payment during the buydown. A future refinance is not a requirement you can safely build your budget around or an outcome anyone can promise. [3]

A permanent buydown involves discount points used to obtain a lower note rate. VA excludes points appropriate to the market from the concession calculation, but extra points for a permanent buydown count toward the cap. We need the lender's actual pricing and classification, not a blanket assumption that all points are outside the limit. [1]

My approach is to compare the available structures against your priorities: keeping cash available after closing, the cost of the points, how long you expect to keep the loan, and comfort with the ongoing housing expense. A larger credit is not automatically a better result if it buys something you do not need.

Do Not Put All Prepaids Outside the Cap

This is an important detail when planning a Florida purchase. VA specifically lists seller prepayment of the buyer's property taxes and insurance as concessions. Calling those items “closing costs” on a worksheet does not move them outside the 4% calculation. [1]

We should review the actual insurance quote, tax estimate, escrow requirements, and closing date before deciding how much help to request. Do not assume every insurance charge or escrow deposit is an excluded ordinary closing cost. The lender needs to classify the actual line items and include the applicable concessions together.

The funding fee needs its own check, too. First, establish whether you owe it. If the seller pays your VA funding fee, that payment counts toward the concession cap. If you are exempt, there is no funding fee to allocate seller money toward. [1] [2]

Build the Request Before Writing the Offer

You do not need to memorize the handbook to use this well. You need a clear plan before your agent negotiates the credit. Here is how I would work through that with you:

  • Start with your goal. Are closing funds the concern, or do you want to compare a buydown with covering other eligible costs? Tell me what would make the purchase workable for you.
  • Build a line-item estimate. Separate ordinary allowable closing costs and market-appropriate points from the concessions that share the 4% limit. Use actual quotes and lender pricing as they become available.
  • Check the lender's rules. VA's baseline is not a promise that every lender offers the same buydown or accepts the same structure. Lender overlays, available pricing, and your qualification still matter.
  • Coordinate with your agent. Match the requested credit to eligible uses, the seller's agreement, and the overall offer. A credit request and a price reduction affect the negotiation differently.
  • Recheck before closing. Reconcile the final costs, credit, and concession total against the NOV. A changed insurance quote, appraisal result, or loan structure can change the allocation.

If you are considering a higher offer price to support a credit, ask us to review that tradeoff first. The seller's agreement does not establish VA reasonable value, and a larger contract price does not automatically enlarge the concession allowance. There also must be enough actual eligible costs to use the credit; do not plan on unused credit becoming spending money.
