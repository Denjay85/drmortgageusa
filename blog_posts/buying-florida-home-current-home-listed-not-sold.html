Buying Before Your Florida Home Sells

By Dennis Ross, NMLS #2018381|October 18, 2026

Your Florida home is listed. You found the next one. Before making an offer, I would ask two separate questions: can your mortgage file work before the sale closes, and where will the purchase money come from?

Do not combine those questions into one assumption that everything will work once the old house sells. For an Orlando move-up buyer, my recommendation is to review the overlap with a loan officer before choosing purchase dates.

Listed Is Not the Same as Pending Sale

For loans following Fannie Mae's rules, when your current principal residence is pending sale but will not transfer to the buyer before your new principal-residence purchase, both the current and proposed housing obligations generally count in qualifying. The guide calls these obligations PITIA.[1]

Fannie Mae provides an exception when the lender has the executed sales contract for the current home and confirmation that any financing contingencies have been cleared. A listing alone does not supply those documents. This is a Fannie Mae rule, not a promise that every loan program or lender handles the situation identically.[1]

I would ask your loan officer: Are we qualifying with both properties? If not, which rule applies, and what evidence is still missing? Get that answer for your actual file instead of assuming a sign in the yard removes the old payment.

Keep Sale Proceeds Separate From Mortgage Qualification

Fannie Mae allows qualification using anticipated sale proceeds but requires documentation of the actual proceeds received. When the sale money is needed for the new down payment and closing costs, the lender must obtain the existing home's settlement statement before or simultaneously with the new settlement. It must show sufficient net cash proceeds for the purchase.[2]

My recommendation: have the lender and settlement professionals explain exactly when your sale funds must be available and how they will document them. Treat a projected sale price as a planning input, not confirmation that the purchase is funded.

I would bring a current mortgage statement, a preliminary seller net sheet if available, your listing or executed sales contract, and the proposed purchase dates to that conversation. Ask which additional records the lender needs. Do not send sensitive financial information through an unsecured message.

Review Three Possible Timelines

I would compare these paths with your loan officer and real estate agent. These are planning options to discuss, not financing approvals or recommendations to sign a particular contract:

  • Sell first: ask what a gap between homes would mean for temporary housing, storage, and the timing of your purchase search.
  • Coordinate the closings: ask the settlement professionals to map the order of events and what happens if either transaction moves.
  • Buy before selling: ask the lender to review qualification and available purchase funds without assuming the sale has already closed.

My preference is to put the plan on paper: which event comes first, which funds are needed next, who confirms receipt, and who you call if something changes. Ask your agent and, when appropriate, a Florida real estate attorney to explain contract contingencies, deadlines, and your obligations. This article does not interpret your contract or tell you to waive protections.

Budget for an Overlap You Can Actually Handle

The CFPB recommends reviewing your finances, deciding what you want to spend, and assembling application paperwork before mortgage shopping.[3] For this move, I would add a separate overlap budget rather than focus only on the eventual new payment.

Write down the bills you expect to pay while you still own the current home. Ask for current estimates for the next property's insurance, taxes, association costs if applicable, and mortgage payment. Then add your own estimates for moving, storage, temporary housing, and a delayed sale.

There is no universal cushion I would prescribe here. Decide what remaining cash would make you comfortable and ask the lender separately about any required reserves. For related planning, read Orlando move-up buyer strategy and mortgage reserves for Florida buyers.

Do Not Add a Backup Loan Without a Fresh Review

If you are considering a bridge loan, HELOC, or another source of purchase funds, bring the proposal to your loan officer before applying or borrowing. Ask how its payment, fees, available proceeds, and payoff plan would affect the mortgage review. Do not assume a product is available for a listed property or that a future refinance will solve the overlap.

For background questions to bring to that discussion, read Florida bridge loan basics. This article does not establish eligibility, pricing, or availability for a bridge loan or credit line.

Before You Commit to the Next Home

I would want clear answers to three questions: Does the loan review assume the current home has sold? Does the purchase depend on receiving its proceeds? What is the plan if the sale is delayed? If an answer changes, ask the loan officer to review the file again before relying on the earlier plan.
