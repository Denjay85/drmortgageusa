Mortgage Shopping Credit Inquiries in Florida

By Dennis Ross, NMLS #2018381|October 24, 2026

Worried that talking to another mortgage lender will wreck your credit? I would not let that fear make the financing decision for an Orlando home. I would first ask what credit check the lender plans to use and organize the comparisons.

The useful distinction is between an inquiry appearing on your report and how a scoring model counts it. Mortgage shopping has special scoring treatment, but that is not a promise that your score cannot change.

Know What the Lender Is Requesting

A hard inquiry from a credit application may affect your score. Checking your own report is a soft inquiry and does not affect your FICO scores. Hard inquiries can remain visible to lenders even when rate-shopping treatment applies.[2]

Before proceeding, I would ask: Is this a soft or hard inquiry? Is it for an initial discussion or an application? Will another credit review be needed later? Write down the answers rather than guessing from the word preapproval.

Why You Hear Both 14 Days and 45 Days

The CFPB explains that inquiries for the same loan type generally receive single-inquiry treatment within a window of 14 to 45 days. Shopping for different loan types is not the same as comparing several mortgage offers.[1]

FICO gives the model-specific explanation: older versions use a 14-day shopping span, while newer versions use 45 days. Those rules concern score calculation, not deletion of separate inquiries from your report.[2]

My recommendation is to prepare your lender shortlist first and aim to concentrate mortgage applications within 14 days when practical. That is a cautious planning choice, not a universal deadline or a guarantee of an unchanged score. Ask each lender which scoring model and process apply to your file.

Do Not Promise Yourself a Particular Point Change

FICO says inquiry impact varies with the person's credit history. I would not promise that a pull costs a fixed number of points, or that shopping means zero impact.[2] If your credit is a concern, ask the loan officer to review your situation before you authorize the next step.

I would also avoid mixing mortgage comparisons with unnecessary credit-card or auto applications. The CFPB distinguishes inquiries for different loan types from same-type shopping.[1] Bring any planned purchase or financing change to your loan officer before acting.

Build a Comparison Plan Before the First Application

Here is the approach I would use with a Florida buyer:

  • Prepare: review your own reports, gather your questions, and choose the lenders you want to compare.
  • Record: note application dates and ask what kind of credit inquiry each step involves.
  • Compare: request written loan terms and ask each lender to explain assumptions, fees, points, credits, and lock status.
  • Follow through: tell your loan officer about new financing plans and ask what must be checked again before closing.

For the offer comparison itself, read our mortgage quote comparison guide. For the starting conversation, see preapproval versus prequalification in Florida.

Ask About the Next Credit Review Too

The CFPB notes that lenders may obtain credit information at application and just before closing.[3] Ask your lender what follow-up review to expect. Do not assume the first pull is the last one, or that a shopping window makes every future inquiry irrelevant.

My advice is to compare deliberately, keep the dates together, and ask questions before authorizing a check. You do not need a promise about your future score to start a useful mortgage conversation.
