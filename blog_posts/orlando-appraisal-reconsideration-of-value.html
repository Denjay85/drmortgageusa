Orlando Appraisal Reconsideration of Value

By Dennis Ross, NMLS #2018381|October 6, 2026

Your Orlando home appraisal came in below the contract price. Before you argue about the number, ask a more useful question: is there a specific error or missing piece of evidence in the report?

My recommendation is to separate two decisions. First, check whether the appraisal deserves a documented review. Second, decide whether the purchase still makes sense if the value does not change. Do not make your entire closing plan depend on winning a challenge.

What Is a Reconsideration of Value?

A reconsideration of value, often called an ROV, asks the lender to review concerns about an appraisal. The CFPB describes factual errors, omissions, inadequate comparable properties, and evidence of prohibited bias as reasons borrowers may raise concerns.[6] It is not a promise of a higher value.

Start with the complete appraisal, not a screenshot of the final number. The CFPB says your lender must provide a copy and cannot interfere with the appraiser's judgment.[4] Ask your loan officer for the lender's current ROV instructions and the procedure that applies to your loan.

Build a Specific Request, Not a Complaint About the Price

For loans subject to Fannie Mae's rules, the request includes the borrower name, property address, appraisal effective date, appraiser name, request date, identified deficiencies, supporting information and sources, and an explanation of why the evidence supports review. Additional comparable properties are limited to five. The lender reviews the request for sufficient detail before sending it to the appraiser.[1]

Here is how I recommend organizing your notes. These are preparation suggestions, not a finding that your report is wrong:

  • Identify the page and item: Point to the exact statement you want reviewed.
  • Explain the concern: Say what appears inaccurate or unsupported, without guessing at the appraiser's motives.
  • Attach the evidence: Ask your agent to help locate records, photographs, or verified sale information relevant to that concern.
  • Explain relevance: Describe why the information matters to this property, rather than just attaching a stack of more expensive homes.

For example, a useful question is, "Does the report's description of the property match the documented features?" A less useful request is, "Please raise the value because we agreed to this price." Keep the submission factual and respectful.

What Makes a Comparable Sale Worth Reviewing?

Fannie Mae says comparable properties should have similar physical and legal characteristics, including site, room count, finished area, style, and condition. The appraiser chooses the most appropriate comparables. Sales in competing market areas can be acceptable with explanation, so distance alone does not establish an error.[3]

For your Orlando purchase, ask your agent to explain why each suggested sale competes with the home you are buying. I would want that explanation beside the sale information, not just a list sorted by highest price.

Ask questions about the neighborhood, property type, condition, features, and transaction timing. Have your lender confirm what supporting data the appraiser can consider. Do not treat an online home-value estimate as your entire submission.

Use the Lender's Process and Protect Appraiser Independence

For consumer credit secured by a principal dwelling, the CFPB's valuation-independence rule permits requests to consider appropriate additional property information, explain a valuation, or correct errors. It prohibits coercion intended to substitute someone else's preferred value for independent judgment.[5]

My recommendation: route the evidence through your lender's designated process. Do not pressure anyone to "hit the contract price." If you suspect discriminatory treatment, describe the concern and supporting evidence to the lender rather than reducing it to a disagreement over price.

Confirm the Loan Rules Before Assuming You Get Another Attempt

Fannie Mae permits one borrower-initiated ROV per appraisal, and its guide does not allow the borrower to submit the request after the loan closes.[1] Ask your lender what happens if the value stays unchanged, including whether any further appraisal review is justified.

These are Fannie Mae requirements, not a blanket statement about every conventional, FHA, VA, or other loan. Ask your loan officer to verify the program-specific procedure, documentation, any applicable costs, and the lender's timing before you act. This article does not claim a universal turnaround time or fee policy.

Keep the Closing Decision Separate From the ROV

The CFPB identifies asking the seller to reduce the price as an option after a low appraisal. It also recommends consulting an attorney about contract and cancellation options.[4] No seller response or contractual remedy is guaranteed.

While the request is reviewed, I recommend asking your team:

  1. What are the actual contract deadlines, and who is verifying them?
  2. What does the lender say happens to this financing if the value stays unchanged?
  3. What would a revised agreement mean for your cash needs and budget?
  4. Do you still want this home if the review does not change the value?

Do not assume an ROV extends a contract deadline or a rate lock. Ask the lender about financing and your agent or attorney about the agreement. For related preparation, read Orlando appraisal gap options and the Florida final walkthrough and closing checklist.

The Bottom Line for Orlando Homebuyers

Challenge a report with evidence, not with the amount you need it to reach. Get the lender's instructions, submit a focused request, and keep an honest backup decision if the value remains unchanged. The goal is a credible appraisal and a purchase you can defend, not a forced closing.
