Earnest Money Documentation for Florida Buyers

By Dennis Ross, NMLS #2018381|October 15, 2026

Before you send an earnest money deposit on a Florida home, ask your lender what records to keep. My recommendation is simple: plan the documentation before the payment, not after a request lands in your inbox.

For an Orlando purchase, I would organize three things separately: where the money came from, evidence of the payment, and confirmation from the person holding it. Then review how the deposit appears in the closing figures. Do not treat the deposit receipt as the entire mortgage file.

Can Earnest Money Count Toward the Purchase?

For loans following Fannie Mae's rules, a sales-contract deposit is an acceptable source for the down payment and closing costs. If it satisfies part of the borrower's minimum contribution, the lender must verify an acceptable source.[1]

That is a Fannie Mae guideline, not a promise about every mortgage. Ask your loan officer which program applies and what your particular lender needs. I would get that answer before deciding which account to use.

What Documentation Does the Lender Review?

Fannie Mae requires bank statements or a verification of deposit to show a two-month average balance sufficient to support the deposit. When a canceled check documents the source, statements must extend through the clearing date. Unclear withdrawals require additional source and transfer evidence. Unusually large deposits need closer evaluation. Receipt is verified with a canceled check or a written statement from the deposit holder.[1]

My recommendation is to ask for a written checklist tailored to your payment method. For a wire or electronic transfer, ask what combination of bank records and deposit-holder confirmation the lender will accept. Do not assume an app screenshot answers every question.

Here is the folder I would prepare for a lender review, with the understanding that the lender decides the actual requirements:

  • Purchase paperwork: the signed contract and any amendments affecting the deposit.
  • Source records: the account statements and any explanation or supporting documents the lender requests.
  • Payment evidence: the check, clearing record, or transfer confirmation appropriate to your method.
  • Receipt: confirmation identifying the deposit holder, property, amount, and payment date.
  • Closing review: the figures showing how the verified deposit is accounted for.

Use the lender's secure upload process for financial records. My preference is to ask which pages are needed rather than crop or redact documents yourself and hope they are usable.

Ask Before Moving Money Between Accounts

If you plan to combine savings, use money from a family member, or pay from a different account than the one already reviewed, discuss that plan with your lender first. Ask what documentation the proposed source would require and whether it is acceptable for your loan.

I would not tell a buyer to move money simply to make a statement look cleaner. Explain the real source and ask how to document it. For related planning, read Florida mortgage gift funds guidance and large bank deposits and mortgage approval.

Check the Deposit Against the Closing Figures

The CFPB's Closing Disclosure explainer separates deposits already paid from the cash still due at closing. The deposit appears among amounts paid already by or for the borrower. Cash to Close is the additional amount needed at closing.[2]

My recommendation is to compare the deposit receipt with the closing figures alongside your loan officer and settlement agent. Ask: Is the correct deposit shown? Are all installments accounted for? What amount remains due? If something looks wrong, request an explanation or correction before sending the final funds.

Do not subtract the deposit a second time from an already adjusted Cash to Close figure. Instead, ask the settlement agent to walk you through the calculation. For a broader preparation list, read the Florida final walkthrough and closing checklist.

Keep Contract Questions Separate From Mortgage Questions

Ask your real estate agent and, when appropriate, a Florida real estate attorney about deposit deadlines, contingencies, cancellation rights, release requirements, or a dispute. This article does not determine who is entitled to the money or whether a deposit is refundable.

For the mortgage conversation, I would stay focused on these questions: Is this source acceptable? What evidence is still missing? Has the deposit been accounted for correctly? What remains subject to underwriting review?

The Bottom Line for Orlando Buyers

Before paying, agree on a documentation plan with your lender and confirm the payment process with your settlement professional. After paying, save the records and review the deposit credit. That is the process I recommend instead of trying to reconstruct everything near closing.
