Orlando Seller Rent-Back and Mortgage Occupancy

By Dennis Ross, NMLS #2018381|November 11, 2026

The seller accepts your offer but asks to stay after closing. Before I would agree, I would want the proposed move-out date in front of the loan officer, the insurance agent, and your Florida real estate attorney. My recommendation for Orlando buyers is to review the possession plan before promising extra time.

This article uses seller rent-back to mean a proposed arrangement where the seller remains in the home after the purchase closes. It is a mortgage planning checklist, not a lease form or legal opinion.

Start With Three Dates, Not a Verbal Promise

I would write down the proposed closing date, the seller's final departure date, and the date you actually intend to move in. Then I would ask the lender to review those dates against the loan's occupancy requirements before the offer is finalized.

My concern is the difference between a plan that sounds convenient and one you can actually carry out. If the seller's next home is not ready, what is your backup? Would you still be comfortable with the purchase if you had to pay for temporary housing or storage? I would budget for that possibility rather than assume an extension will be harmless.

Why the Mortgage Occupancy Review Matters

For loans covered by Fannie Mae's Selling Guide, primary-residence treatment includes moving into and using the home within 60 days of signing the relevant security instrument. The guide calls for at least a year of occupancy starting when you move in, subject to its permitted exceptions and the instrument's terms.[1]

That is a Fannie Mae requirement, not a statement that every mortgage follows identical rules. It is also not a blanket approval for a seller to stay for the entire period. My recommendation is to ask the lender to review the exact proposed agreement, the actual move-in plan, and any lender-specific requirements. Do not choose a rent-back length by copying an internet rule.

VA Buyers Should Have the Same Conversation Early

If you are buying with a VA loan, my recommendation is to have your loan officer review the proposed possession dates and your actual move-in plan before you agree. I would not assume a conventional-loan guideline answers the VA question.

Do not assume military status, a seller's request, or a planned extension resolves the occupancy question. For related background, see VA occupancy requirements for Florida buyers.

Keep Contract Advice Separate From Loan Review

The Florida Bar's homebuying guide recommends consulting a Florida real estate lawyer before signing a purchase contract. Its checklist specifically asks when possession will occur, whether an occupancy agreement has been prepared by a licensed Florida lawyer when possession differs from closing, and who is responsible for insurance.[2]

I would take these questions to your own attorney and agent:

  • What exact date and time ends the seller's permitted stay?
  • How should payment, utilities, maintenance, property condition, and access be addressed?
  • Would a deposit or holdback be appropriate, and who would administer it?
  • What happens if the seller does not leave or the home is damaged?

These are discussion questions, not proposed contract terms. I am not prescribing a deposit amount, penalty, legal classification, or removal procedure. Have your attorney explain your rights, obligations, and remedies under the actual documents. A lender's review is not a substitute for that advice.

Ask the Insurance Professional About the Actual Arrangement

My recommendation is to describe the seller's proposed stay to the licensed insurance agent before binding coverage. Ask which policy and effective dates fit the facts, what the carrier needs to know, and whether the proposed arrangement meets the lender's insurance requirements. Do not assume a policy quoted for your immediate move-in answers a different occupancy plan.

I would request written confirmation of the coverage arrangement rather than rely on a conversation passed through several people. This article does not determine what any particular policy covers or whether a claim would be paid.

Put the Possession Plan Into Your Closing Preparation

Before closing, I would reconcile the dates across the documents and ask the loan officer what needs another review if anything changes. I would also discuss how property condition will be documented before closing and at possession, instead of assuming a single walkthrough settles both questions. For broader preparation, read the Florida final walkthrough and mortgage closing checklist.

If a change is proposed late, my recommendation is to stop and get the relevant professionals back into the conversation. I would compare the proposed stay with the alternative of negotiating a different closing date, without assuming either option is available or better for every buyer.

My bottom line: I would not treat a seller rent-back as a minor favor. Get the actual dates reviewed, have your own attorney handle the agreement, and have the insurance professional confirm the coverage plan before you commit.
