First Mortgage Payment After Closing in Florida

By Dennis Ross, NMLS #2018381|October 27, 2026

Before you spend the money left after an Orlando home closing, I would put the first mortgage payment on your calendar. Start with the actual due date and payment instructions in your closing package, not a friend's experience or a rule of thumb.

Here is the distinction I want Florida buyers to understand: time before the first scheduled payment is not the same thing as free interest. Prepaid interest can be collected at closing for the gap before the period covered by that payment.[2]

When Is the First Mortgage Payment Due?

With a typical monthly mortgage schedule, interest is paid in arrears, meaning the payment includes interest for the month that just ended. The University of California's loan-program office illustrates a loan funded October 17, with interest collected through October 31 at closing and a first payment due December 1 covering November interest.[1]

That is a timing example, not a promised due date for your loan or a Florida-specific rule. Ask your lender to confirm the date in your signed documents and the instructions for making that payment. If the closing or funding date changes, ask whether anything needs to be updated.

Prepaid Interest Is Not a Free Month

The CFPB describes prepaid interest as daily interest due at closing between closing and the period covered by the first monthly payment. Look for it on page 2, Section F, of the Loan Estimate and Closing Disclosure. The amount can change between those documents.[2]

I would ask the loan officer to walk through which dates that line covers. Then ask which interest period the first regular payment covers. This is a better conversation than asking whether you get to skip a payment.

Separate Closing Money From the Monthly Budget

The Closing Disclosure distinguishes principal and interest, estimated escrow, and the estimated total monthly payment. Escrow can include property taxes and homeowners insurance. The initial escrow deposit at closing establishes an account balance; it is a separate line from prepaid interest.[3]

My recommendation is to keep three separate planning lists: money needed to close, money reserved for the first scheduled payment, and moving or setup expenses. Do not treat whatever remains in checking as a furniture budget.

Ask what is outside the mortgage payment too. The CFPB notes that HOA fees are often not included in escrow.[3] I would list association bills, utilities, maintenance, and any rent overlap separately, then use your actual bills and agreements to fill in the amounts.

Confirm Who Gets the First Payment

The company collecting your mortgage payments is the servicer, and that company can change. CFPB guidance says servicing-transfer notices identify when the old servicer stops accepting payments and the new one starts. Bank bill-pay instructions may need updating, and borrowers should review statements to confirm payments are credited accurately.[4]

Before the due date, I would confirm the servicer, account information, payment method, and whether an automatic payment is actually scheduled. If an online account is not ready, contact the lender or servicer through verified contact information and ask how to pay on time. Do not assume an unfinished login setup changes the due date.

My First-Payment Checklist

  • Get the exact first due date and payment instructions in writing.
  • Review prepaid interest and the initial escrow deposit with the loan officer.
  • Confirm the total scheduled payment and expenses paid separately.
  • Keep the payment money separate from moving purchases.
  • Check that the payment was received and applied, rather than relying only on a scheduled-transfer screen.

I would finish this checklist before closing day, then check it again after receiving any servicing notice. The goal is simple: know what you owe, when it is due, and where it goes.
