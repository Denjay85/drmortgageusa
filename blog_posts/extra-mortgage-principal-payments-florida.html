Extra Mortgage Principal Payments in Florida

By Dennis Ross, NMLS #2018381|November 2, 2026

You send extra money to your mortgage, then open the next statement expecting a smaller payment. The balance is lower. The amount due looks the same. For an Orlando homeowner, those are two different goals, and I would separate them before moving the money.

The CFPB explains that a typical fixed-rate mortgage keeps the same combined principal and interest payment while the split between those two parts changes. Paying down principal reduces what you owe; a lower balance means less interest as the loan amortizes.[1] That is not the same as receiving a new required payment.

Start With the Result You Actually Want

My first question is not how much extra you can send. It is what you need the money to accomplish. Are you trying to reduce the debt, make your monthly budget easier, or keep cash available for repairs and a possible move?

I would not use a balance-reduction plan as a substitute for a confirmed payment-reduction plan. Keep budgeting for the amount your servicer requires unless it confirms a change.

Confirm How to Request Principal-Only Application

The Office of the Comptroller of the Currency recommends checking your loan agreement or contacting your bank for its specific extra-principal process.[2] Before sending money, ask your servicer which payment field or instruction identifies an additional principal payment.

For current loans covered by its guide, Fannie Mae requires servicers to accept and apply additional principal identified by the borrower. Its guidance treats delinquent loans differently: extra principal first goes toward curing the delinquency.[3] This is not a blanket promise about every mortgage or account status.

My recommendation is to save the confirmation and compare it with the next statement. Ask the servicer to explain any application you do not recognize. Do not guess that an extra transfer replaces the next scheduled payment.

A Recast Is a Separate Question

Fannie Mae's servicing guide includes a re-amortization process for a borrower requesting a lower contractual monthly payment after a substantial principal reduction. It identifies documentation and reporting steps for that process.[3] Sending the money and obtaining that payment change are separate steps.

Ask whether your specific loan permits a recast, what amount and fee would apply, what documents are needed, and when a new payment would take effect. Get the answer before making a large transfer if a lower required payment is the reason you are doing it. This article does not establish recast eligibility for any government-backed or other loan.

For the broader comparison, read mortgage recast versus refinance in Florida. Start with the goal, then confirm which option your loan actually supports.

Check Restrictions Before Making a Large Payment

The CFPB says some loans have prepayment penalties, and a large partial payoff can sometimes trigger one. Small extra-principal payments normally do not, but borrowers should verify their terms.[4] Ask your servicer to identify any restriction or charge that applies to the payment you are considering. Do not treat a general article as a review of your note.

Keep Your Full Housing Budget in the Conversation

The CFPB notes that mortgage payments often include taxes and insurance in addition to principal and interest.[1] A discussion about the loan balance is not a review of those bills. For that separate issue, see Florida escrow shortages and payment changes.

My recommendation: review your latest statement, the extra-principal instructions, any recast terms, and the cash you want to keep available before choosing an amount. I would rather help a homeowner make a clear decision than have them move cash based on a payment reduction nobody confirmed.
